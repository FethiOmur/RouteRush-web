The difference

A tracker tells you where you went.
RouteRush tells you what you took.

The same run, scored differently. Distance and pace are the receipt. The map is the score — a permanent record of the streets you have actually covered, block by block, against everyone else covering theirs.

Ground taken

The map remembers every street you have taken.

Close a loop on your run and everything inside it becomes yours, in the shape of the streets you took. Come back a different way and the shape of what you own changes.

Distance earns, ground earns more. Every kilometre earns, a running streak multiplies it, and new ground earns on top — only ground you did not already hold, so the same loop cannot be farmed.

Ground changes hands. Nobody wears your claim down — they take it. A rival who closes a loop over your ground has the overlap cut out the moment their run saves.

It compounds. Territory carries between runs, so a neighbourhood is something you build over months.

Boldi, the in-run coach

A coach in your ear, not a podcast.

Boldi watches the run as it happens — your pace, the ground ahead, how much you have left — and, on a coached run, says something when it is worth saying. The rest of the time he is quiet, so your music keeps playing.

Timed to the effort. Prompts arrive at splits and decision points, not on a fixed timer.

Aware of the map. He knows which ground is still unclaimed on the route in front of you.

Leagues

Thirty runners.
One week.
Five go up.

Every week you join a league at your tier, up to thirty runners. The top sixth goes up and the bottom sixth goes down — five each way in a full league — and everyone starts again on Monday. Three scoring runs is the price of going up. Bronze, Silver and Gold, each with three divisions.

New to a tier? A newbie shield covers your first week, so a bad one cannot send you straight back down.
